Practice a round: a 45-minute timer for mobile system design

Pick a prompt. Start the clock. Practise out loud, one step at a time, and see how long each step lasts.

How it works

  1. Pick a case or press Pick a case for me. Pick your level.
  2. Start the timer. The page shows the step you are in and a short reminder for it.
  3. Write notes in the box if you want. They stay in this tab only.
  4. At the end, tick what you covered. You get a short summary and links to read more.

The five steps and their minutes are the same as the five-step method. The time split is my suggestion, not a rule from any company.

Your data stays here. Nothing is sent to a server. Nothing is saved. If you close the tab, your notes are gone.

    Without JavaScript

    The timer needs JavaScript. If it is off, or if you prefer a plain clock, use this table. Start your own clock, then move on when the minutes for a step are used. The table is also a good page to print.

    StepDo thisAsk yourself
    1. Scope and constraints
    4 minutes    		Ask five questions and name what is out of scope. Say what the phone does to the design.What is in scope, and what is out? What does the phone do to this design?
    2. Data and contracts
    10 minutes    		Say where the data lives and which copy the app trusts. Name the IDs and the calls to the server.Which copy does the app trust? What ID makes a retry safe?
    3. Client structure
    8 minutes    		Say who owns the work: the screen, the ViewModel, or a service that lives longer.Who owns the work, and does it live longer than the screen?
    4. Make the hard part work
    18 minutes    		Walk the hard path slowly. Say the first idea, why it fails, and what you do instead.What is my first idea? Why does it fail? What do I do instead?
    5. Break it, ship it, roll it back
    5 minutes    		Say what can fail and what the user sees. Say how you turn the change off and how you roll it back.What fails, and what does the user see? How do I turn it off and roll it back?

    Total: 45 minutes. Make the hard part work gets the biggest block on purpose.

    Practice kit

    Five free pages for a practice round with a friend, or on your own. Use them in this order: see a strong answer, make a plan, practise with a friend, then score yourself. The quick drills are for a short 10 minute round on any case. The first four use the chat app case.

    1. Weak and strong answer
      One chat app answer, weak and strong, side by side, and what the weak one skips.
    2. Study plans
      A 3-week plan and a 4-week plan, each in 2, 5 and 10 hour sizes.
    3. Mock interview script
      A 45-minute script for a round with a friend: the prompt, the time calls and six follow-up questions.
    4. Score sheet
      Six rows, scores 1 to 4, to rate a practice round. You can print it.
    5. Quick drills
      29 short practice prompts with what to ask first and the case to read next.

Where to start

Pick the track closest to your level. Each track is a reading path through the case studies and guides, in order. Each step gives the reason to read it, the reading time, and what the page helps you say. The levels are the author's own groups, not an official scale. You can read in any order. Everything is free to read, with no signup.

Mid-level track

Core app skills: saving data on the device first, safe retries, paging and caching. About 93 minutes of reading in total.

  1. The five-step method4 minThe method that every other page uses, and how the 45 minutes are spent.You will be able to: explain the five steps and how to split the time in a round.
  2. Design a chat app15 minThe main offline example: outbox, safe retries (idempotent sends) and sync.You will be able to: explain why a send is saved on the device first, and how a retry avoids a duplicate.
  3. Design a news feed15 minBuilds on the chat app: paging, optimistic writes and fresh cached data.You will be able to: describe how a feed loads pages, shows a change before the server confirms it, and stays fresh.
  4. Design a pagination library15 minPaging as a reusable library: public API, generic types and cancelling requests.You will be able to: design the public API of a paging library and say what stays private.
  5. Design an image loading library15 minCaches, decoding and cancelling requests for a scrolling list.You will be able to: explain how an image loader builds cache keys, caches images, shrinks them and cancels requests.
  6. Design photo backup and file sync14 minUploads that can resume, and who runs the transfer in the background.You will be able to: explain uploads that resume by block, and which system part schedules them.
  7. Design a hotel or ride booking app15 minHolds, server time and payments that are safe to retry.You will be able to: describe a hold that ends on server time and a payment retry that cannot charge twice.

Senior track

Trade-offs and failure modes in questions about sync and about SDKs (libraries that other apps use). About 192 minutes of reading in total.

  1. The five-step method4 minThe method, and a time split to practise with.You will be able to: explain the five steps and say where the time goes.
  2. Design a chat app15 minStart with the lost acknowledgement example.You will be able to: explain the outbox and the idempotency key, and what happens when an acknowledgement is lost.
  3. Offline-first mobile system design: one model, six case studies11 minThe one model behind six of the case studies.You will be able to: explain the offline-first model and choose a conflict policy for each kind of data.
  4. Design a news feed15 minOptimistic writes, and marking cached data as old, when something fails.You will be able to: defend a paging choice and a choice about when cached data becomes old, and say how each can fail.
  5. Design photo backup and file sync14 minThe hardest sync problem here: blocks, conflicts and background limits.You will be able to: explain sync by blocks, conflicts and the background limits of each platform.
  6. Design a travel plans app15 minOffline editing by several people: the server decides one field at a time, and a time keeps the clock of its place.You will be able to: explain how edits merge field by field, and why a companion's change is never lost without a sign.
  7. Design a hotel or ride booking app15 minTime that the server controls, and recovery after a payment problem.You will be able to: explain holds, booking that is safe to retry, and how the app recovers after a crash.
  8. Design a state machine for a mobile flow14 minOne list of states and events for a checkout, and what the app does when it is killed mid-payment.You will be able to: draw the states of a flow, explain why a disabled button is not a guard, and recover from a lost payment answer.
  9. Design a stock ticker app14 minThe always-online contrast: streaming updates, joining updates (conflation) and resync.You will be able to: design a price stream that ignores old updates, finds gaps and syncs again.
  10. Design a public transport appFree teaser, 6 minFree teaser. A large offline timetable with live delays merged on top; the full case is planned for the study pack.You will be able to: split a timetable (static) from live data, and say how old data is shown to the user.
  11. Design a video streaming app15 minPlayback state, loading ahead and cost. It builds on the feed and image loading pages.You will be able to: explain which part owns the player, how far ahead to prefetch and what that costs.
  12. Design a push notification SDK15 minSDK design: token life cycle, delivery and app states.You will be able to: explain token refresh, delivery limits and what changes in each app state.
  13. Design an analytics SDK14 minSDK design with limits: batching, saving to disk and privacy.You will be able to: design an event queue that survives when the system ends the app process, and respects user consent.
  14. Design feature flags and remote config15 minA client that must start without the network: saved values, a stable rollout and a kill switch.You will be able to: explain why new values wait for the next session, and how a kill switch still reaches the app.
  15. Staff-level mobile system design: what the round tests10 minOptional: see what changes at staff level.You will be able to: separate what a senior answer covers from what a staff answer adds.

Staff track

Models that cover many cases, decisions for the whole platform, and how to present them. About 161 minutes of reading in total.

  1. The five-step method4 minThe method, and a time split to practise with.You will be able to: explain the five steps and say where the time goes.
  2. Design a chat app15 minThe worked example that the staff guide builds on.You will be able to: explain the outbox and the send that is safe to retry, before adding staff-level scope.
  3. Offline-first mobile system design: one model, six case studies11 minThe model that ties the case studies together.You will be able to: use one offline-first model for chat, feed, upload, booking, analytics and push.
  4. Design photo backup and file sync14 minConflicts, versioning and background transfer limits.You will be able to: explain conflict policy and transfer scheduling on both platforms.
  5. Design a stock ticker app14 minUsed in week 2 of the staff practice plan: cost, gaps and resync.You will be able to: explain the cost of a feed and how the app finds and repairs a gap.
  6. Design an analytics SDK14 minSDK ownership: versions, remote settings and more than one SDK.You will be able to: talk about SDK versions, privacy and rollout, not only the queue.
  7. Design feature flags and remote config15 minThe release tool itself: who owns the flag list, flag lifetimes and the kill switch.You will be able to: explain how a risky change is released behind a flag, watched, and switched off.
  8. Design a push notification SDK15 minPlatform differences, working with other push handlers, and failure testing.You will be able to: explain the token life cycle and how your SDK works with other push handlers.
  9. Design a hotel or ride booking app15 minUsed in week 4 of the staff practice plan: payments behind feature flags, modules and testing.You will be able to: explain how a change to the payment path is released behind a feature flag, and how modules are split between teams.
  10. Design a video streaming app15 minUsed in week 4 of the staff practice plan: who owns the player, CDN (servers close to users) cost, and user groups (cohorts).You will be able to: weigh CDN data cost against engagement, and say how player changes are compared between cohorts.
  11. Staff-level mobile system design: what the round tests10 minWhat staff answers add, and a four-week practice plan.You will be able to: say what changes from senior to staff and how you would practise it.
  12. Native vs cross-platform: write the decision record19 minEnds the track: a decision record for choosing a framework.You will be able to: write a decision record with the assumption that would change the choice, a migration path and a rollback.

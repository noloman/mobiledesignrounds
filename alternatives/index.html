You have several ways to prepare for a mobile system design interview. Some are books. Some are courses. Some are free pages. This page puts the ones I could check side by side, so you can pick what fits you.

A note first: I wrote one of these options

In short: I am the author of Mobile Design Rounds. This page is written by one of the options, so read it with that in mind.

I am Manuel Lorenzo, an Android developer. I want you to find the right resource, even when it is not mine. I do not say that any option is better or worse. I say what each one offers, what it costs on its own page, and who I think it fits.

Two tags mark every fact. Verified means I read the official page on 2026-10-14 and the link goes to it. Opinion means it is my own view. Prices change, so check the page before you pay.

Where the others are stronger

In short: Several options have things I do not have: people, video, brand, more topics and a long track record.

  • Human feedback. Exponent says it offers mock interviews with coaches. Hello Interview Premium offers guided practice with feedback. I have no mock interviews and no human reviewer for your answers. [Opinion] A person who watches you answer is hard to replace.
  • Scale and brand. ByteByteGo and DesignGurus are known brands. DesignGurus shows 519 learners on its mobile course. My site is new and has no track record.
  • More topics in one place. The two big course platforms also sell backend system design. Verified: DesignGurus pricing page, checked 2026-10-14 lists several backend courses. If your loop has a backend round too, one account can cover both.
  • Video and recordings. The weeeBox repository links to a YouTube archive of mock interviews. I have no video at all. I could not check any other YouTube channel, so I name none.
  • Quizzes and scored practice. Educative lists 101 quizzes and 3 scored AI mock interviews. My practice tools are small simulators and a timer.
  • Architecture at book length. Tjeerd in 't Veen's books run to 30 chapters. [Opinion] They fit someone who wants depth on how to organise a large app, more than a 45-minute answer.

Where Mobile Design Rounds is different

In short: It is free, covers both platforms, and writes each case as an interview answer. It is also new, with one author.

  • Free. The cases and build guides cost nothing. I plan a paid pack later. It does not exist yet.
  • Interview first. Each case shows the first idea, why it fails, and what to say. A failure table lists what the user sees. See the chat app failure table for an example.
  • Both platforms. The Android parts follow the official Android documentation. I write the iOS parts from Apple's documentation.
  • Build guides and simulators. You can read the schema and code, and then change one thing in a simulator.
  • Plain English. I keep sentences short, and a script checks them before each release.
  • Dated checks. Each case says what I checked in official documents and what I did not.

My limits matter as much. The site is new. One person writes it. I draft it with AI assistance and then edit and review it myself. No outside iOS engineer has reviewed the iOS parts yet. There are no mock interviews and no video.

How to choose

In short: Pick by what you lack most: feedback, depth, breadth or price.

  1. You want a person to judge your answers. Look at a coach-led service such as Exponent or a guided practice plan such as Hello Interview Premium. Use my timer only for solo practice.
  2. You have a few weeks and no budget. Start with the free options: my cases, the weeeBox repository and the free Hello Interview guide. [Opinion] Mix at least two. Then one author's blind spots do not decide your answer.
  3. You like quizzes and a course player. The Educative and DesignGurus courses are built that way.
  4. You want a book to read on your own time. Compare the ByteByteGo book, the Leanpub book and the $89 bundle. Read the sample chapters first.
  5. You also have a backend design round. A general system design course covers that part. None of the mobile options replace it.
  6. You want to see how real companies build. Read engineering blogs. They teach real systems, but they do not teach how to answer in 45 minutes.

[Opinion] Whatever you choose, practise out loud. Reading is not the same skill as answering.
